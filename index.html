      Section 2 · 12.1

      Population parameters and sample statistics

      Studying every individual is often impractical. Sampling selects part of the population; statistical inference uses that sample to draw conclusions about the population.

      Population→ sampling →Sample→ inference →Population

      Population parameter

      A numerical measure calculated from the whole population, such as \(\mu\) or \(\sigma^2\). Usually fixed but unknown.

      Sample statistic

      A numerical measure calculated from sample data, such as \(\bar x\) or \(s^2\). It varies between samples.

      Try it

      Classify each numerical measure

      Simple random sampling

      In a simple random sample, every unit has an equal chance of selection. In this chapter, random samples are drawn with replacement unless otherwise stated, so observations are independent and identically distributed.

      The sampling distribution of the sample mean

      Sample mean as a random variable\[\bar X=\frac{X_1+X_2+\cdots+X_n}{n}\]

      Different random samples generally produce different values of \(\bar X\). Its probability distribution is called its sampling distribution.

      Theorem 12.1

      For a random sample of size \(n\) from a population with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\):

      \[E(\bar X)=\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\]

      The standard error of the mean is \(\sigma/\sqrt n\).

      Do not mix them up

      \(\sigma^2\) is the population variance. \(\sigma^2/n\) is the variance of \(\bar X\). Later, \(S^2\) is an estimator of \(\sigma^2\), not the variance of \(\bar X\).

      Section 4 · 12.2C

      Sample means from a normal population

      Normal population

      If \(X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), then for any sample size \(n\),

      \[\bar X\sim N\left(\mu,\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)\]

      Standardize using the standard error:

      \[Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\sim N(0,1)\]

      Exact, not approximate

      When the population itself is normal, the sample mean is exactly normal for every \(n\), even \(n=2\). The \(n\ge30\) guideline belongs to the CLT for non-normal populations.

      Section 5 · 12.2D

      Sample means from a non-normal population

      Theorem 12.2 · Central Limit Theorem

      For a non-normal population with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), when \(n\) is sufficiently large:

      \[\bar X\stackrel{\text{approx.}}{\sim}N\left(\mu,\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)\]

      The textbook’s practical guideline is \(n\ge30\); strongly skewed populations may need more.

      Section 6 · 12.3

      Point estimation and unbiased estimators

      Point estimation

      An estimator \(\hat\theta\) is a sample statistic used to estimate a population parameter \(\theta\). Its observed value is a point estimate. It is unbiased if \(E(\hat\theta)=\theta\).

      \[\hat\mu=\bar X,\qquad \hat\sigma^2=S^2=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2}{n-1}=\frac{\sum X_i^2-n\bar X^2}{n-1}\]
      Why n − 1?

      Dividing \(\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2\) by \(n\) underestimates \(\sigma^2\) on average. Its expectation is \((n-1)\sigma^2/n\); dividing by \(n-1\) corrects this bias.

      Although \(E(S^2)=\sigma^2\), generally \(E(S)\ne\sigma\). Nevertheless, the textbook uses \(s\) as the point estimate of \(\sigma\).

      Section 7 · 12.4A–B(I)

      Confidence intervals with known variance

      A point estimate gives one number. A confidence interval gives a range built from that estimate. The confidence level describes the long-run success rate of the method.

      Simulate

      Which intervals capture the true μ = 100?

      Interpret carefully

      The population mean is fixed. Before sampling, the random interval has a 95% chance of containing \(\mu\). After calculating one interval, we say we are 95% confident in the method—not that μ randomly moves into the interval.

      Normal population · known σ\[\left(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\ \bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\right)\]

      Critical values: 90% → 1.645, 95% → 1.96, 99% → 2.575.

      Play

      Control centre, width and confidence

      Make an interval narrower

      Increase \(n\), decrease \(\sigma\), or lower the confidence level. In practice, increasing sample size is usually the controllable choice. Width \(=2z_{\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n\).

      Section 8 · 12.4B(II)

      Confidence intervals with unknown variance

      Any population · unknown variance · n ≥ 30

      Replace the unknown \(\sigma\) by the sample standard deviation \(s\). By the CLT, an approximate interval is

      \[\left(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n},\ \bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}\right)\]
      Calculate

      Use sample sums to build an interval

      Known versus unknown

      Use \(\sigma\) only when the population standard deviation is known. When it is estimated from the sample, use \(s\) and check the large-sample condition \(n\ge30\).

      Section 9 · Summary

      Summary and notes

      Sample mean

      \[E(\bar X)=\mu,\quad \operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n},\quad SE=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\]

      Normal population

      \[\bar X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2/n),\quad Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\]

      Central Limit Theorem

      \[\bar X\stackrel{approx.}{\sim}N(\mu,\sigma^2/n),\quad n\ge30\]

      Point estimates

      \[\hat\mu=\bar x,\quad s^2=\frac{\sum x_i^2-n\bar x^2}{n-1}\]

      CI · known σ

      \[\left(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\right)\]

      Approximate CI · unknown σ

      \[\left(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n},\bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}\right),\ n\ge30\]
